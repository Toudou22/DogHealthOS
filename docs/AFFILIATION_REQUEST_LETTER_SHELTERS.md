### UNMATCHED FEATURES TAILORED FOR SHELTERS & RESCUE GROUPS

DogHealthOS provides powerful, clinical-grade tools engineered specifically to support rescued dogs during their critical 3-3-3 transition period (3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to learn routine, 3 months to feel at home):

#### 1. Co-Branded Digital "Adoption Audio Passport"
When an adopter takes home a rescue dog, they receive a personalized digital health passport pre-configured with their dog's breed mix, estimated age, and medical background:
> **In-App & Audio Feature:**  
> 🎙️ **Adoption Transition Audio Guide (Voiced by Dr. Emma Foster, DVM)**  
> *"Congratulations on adopting [Dog Name] from [Shelter Name]! To ensure a seamless 3-3-3 adoption transition, here are your personalized feeding guidelines, decompressing tips, and vaccine schedule recommended by [Shelter Name]..."*

#### 2. Emergency Triage & Symptom Guardrails (Zero Panic, Fewer Shelter Calls)
New adopters often panic over minor post-adoption adjustments (e.g., loose stool from diet change, mild stress lethargy) or miss urgent clinical warnings. 
- **AI Symptom Triage**: Gives adopters immediate red-flag indicators (Red / Orange / Yellow / Green triage levels) with clear instructions on when to contact a vet vs. monitor at home.
- **Reduces Unnecessary Calls**: Filters non-emergency adopter queries while safeguarding pet health.

#### 3. Dietary Stabilization & BCS Weight Recovery Tracking
Rescue dogs frequently arrive underweight or require dietary transition from shelter kibble to home food.
- **NRC Caloric & Portion Calculator**: Calculates precise daily calories based on age, neuter status, and target Body Condition Score (BCS 1-9).
- **WALTHAM Growth & Recovery Curves**: Helps adopters monitor healthy weight gain for rescued puppies or undernourished rescues.

#### 4. Lifetime Digital Health Vault & Vaccine Reminders
Keep adopters on track with spay/neuter follow-ups, booster vaccinations, and heartworm/flea preventatives. Adopters can export complete clinical PDFs to share with their private veterinarian at their first check-up.
